CraneForge SOP

Standard operating procedure — everything you need to build a print-ready crane kit, start to finish.
CraneForge takes your finished artwork and formats it into a complete decal kit sized for a real machine. It does not create art — you bring the graphics, it handles sizing, shaping, layout, and export. Work top to bottom: name the kit, load art into panels, set real sizes, arrange, then export. Sections below expand — read the Quick Start first, dip into the rest as needed.
Quick Start (the 60-second version)
  • 1. Name your kit (top bar) and confirm DPI is 300 unless your printer needs otherwise.
  • 2. Stage your art in the 🎟️ Merch Room (the strip under the top bar) — drop in all the images for this job at once.
  • 3. Drag art onto panels — pull a piece from the Merch Room straight onto any panel. (Or upload directly into a panel.)
  • 4. Set each panel's real size in inches. Pixels fill in automatically. Use the Fit buttons to tile panels flush.
  • 5. Shape & edit if needed — pick a shape, open Edit to zoom/crop/rotate/recolor.
  • 6. Arrange in the Sheet Preview — drag to reposition, resize, swap.
  • 7. Check it with View finished.
  • 8. Export — pick what you need (PNGs, print-ready PDF, tiled sheets), download, print.
Save your work with 💾 Save — to the in-app library, or as a portable .craneforge file you can keep on your desktop or a share drive. Use Ctrl+Z to undo anything.
1 · Kit setup name + DPI

Kit name — name it after the machine or promo it's for (e.g. "Halloween Crane — Front Wall"). This names your saved kit and the exported ZIP.

DPI (dots per inch) is the print resolution. 300 is the standard for sharp large-format prints — leave it there unless your printer or print shop tells you otherwise. DPI is what links inches to pixels: at 300 DPI, an 8-inch panel is 2400 pixels.

Set DPI before sizing panels. Changing it later rescales the pixel dimensions.
2 · Panels & artwork upload, rename, add/remove

The kit starts with the 7 standard crane panels: Marquee Header, Left & Right Side Banners, Main Front Panel, Logo Badge, Prize Out Sign, and a blank Tag.

  • Upload art: click a panel's image area and choose your file. Click Replace on a filled panel to swap it.
  • Rename a panel: click its name and type. Useful when a machine's layout doesn't match the defaults.
  • Add a panel: + Add panel beneath the panel list for extra pieces.
  • Remove: the 🗑 deletes a custom panel; ✕ clears just the image.
  • Collapse: the ▲ on each panel folds it up to reduce clutter.
Bring the highest-resolution art you have. CraneForge can size art down cleanly, but blowing a small image up to a big panel will look pixelated when printed.
🎟️ Merch Room stage & drag-place art

The Merch Room is the strip just under the top bar — your staging area for all the artwork in a job before it goes on a panel. It's the fastest way to work.

  • Add art: click + Add art (or drag image files onto the strip). Add as many as you want — stage the whole job at once.
  • Place it: drag a thumbnail straight onto any panel — either the panel card up top or its spot in the Sheet Preview. The panel highlights teal as you hover; release to drop the art in.
  • Remove a piece: hover a thumbnail and click ✕.
  • Click the Merch Room header to collapse or expand the strip.
Think of it like a prize chute of artwork: load it up, then grab and drop pieces wherever you need them. The Merch Room saves and loads with your kit.
3 · Dimensions inches, pixels, ratio lock

Each panel has W (width) and H (height) in both inches and pixels. Type in either — the other updates live, and the preview resizes as you go. Press Enter to commit.

Always work in inches — they're the real-world size the panel prints at. Measure the machine's actual surface and enter that.

  • 🔗 Lock-ratio (next to the W field): when ON, width and height scale together so the panel keeps its proportions — change one, the other follows. When OFF (default), they're independent. Turn it on when you want to resize without distorting the art's aspect.
  • ↺ Reset appears when a panel differs from its default size — click to restore it.
  • Fit buttons (under the H field): one-click tiling helpers that do the math for you. A side banner shows ↕ Fill column height (sizes it to match header + main stacked) and ⧉ Match other side. The header and main panel show ↔ Match width to each other. Use these to make panels line up flush with no gaps.
For a clean, gap-free finished layout, panel sizes need to fit together — e.g. the side banners as tall as the header + main panel stacked. The Fit buttons do exactly this in one click, or measure the real machine and enter the numbers directly.
4 · Shapes 14 options

Each panel can be cut to a shape: rectangle, rounded, arch, oval, circle, diamond, hexagon, star, shield, cloud, wave, badge, pennant, and more. Use the shape row under each panel — the ‹ › arrows scroll through them.

The shape clips both the on-screen preview and the exported file, so what you see is what prints.

Most crane panels are plain rectangles. Shapes shine for badges, prize signs, and decorative tags.
5 · Edit image zoom, crop, rotate, flip, color

Open the Edit image tray on any panel for:

  • Zoom — scale the art inside the panel frame.
  • Rotate — spin the image in degrees.
  • Brightness / Contrast / Saturation — quick color correction.
  • Flip H / Flip V — mirror horizontally or vertically.
  • ✂ Crop — opens a window; drag a box over the part of the image to keep, then Apply crop. Clear crop removes it. Crop clears automatically if you upload a new image or hit Reset.

To reposition art inside a panel, drag the image directly in the Sheet Preview. Double-click to re-center.

All edits are baked into the export — the printed panel matches the preview exactly.
6 · Locks panel lock vs ratio lock
  • 🔒 Panel lock — freezes a panel you're happy with so its size and art can't be changed by accident. Click again to unlock. Each panel locks independently.
  • 🔗 Ratio lock — different thing: keeps W and H proportional while you resize (see Dimensions).
7 · Sheet Preview arrange, resize, swap

The Sheet Preview shows the whole kit assembled to scale.

  • Drag an image to reposition it within its panel.
  • Drag the ⤡ corner to resize a panel continuously.
  • Double-click an image to center it.
  • Drag the ⠿ handle from one panel onto another to swap their full contents — art, size, shape, edits, everything.

The preview scales everything to fit the available width, so nothing runs off the edge.

8 · View finished final proof

View finished shows the complete kit at true proportions, scaled down so the whole thing fits on screen with nothing cropped. This is your proof — what the assembled wrap actually looks like.

Big layouts display smaller; that's expected. Use browser or phone zoom to inspect detail.

Empty gaps in the finished view mean the panel sizes don't tile together. That's a sizing choice, not a bug — adjust dimensions if you want them tight.
9 · Crane Mockup preview on a real machine

Test how a panel looks on the actual machine before printing:

  • Upload a photo of the real crane.
  • Pick a panel from the dropdown and click Place selected panel.
  • A box drops on the photo with 4 draggable teal corners — drag them to the corners of the surface you're wrapping. The decal warps to fit (corner-pin perspective).
For a 3/4-angle photo where the machine turns a corner, place a Front panel and a Side panel separately — a flat decal can't bend around a corner, so fake the wrap with two pieces.
10 · Print canvas printer presets & fit

Set your printer under Printer preset: Letter (8.5×11, default), Legal, Tabloid, Super B, plotter rolls (24/36/44"), or Custom.

A live badge shows ✓ Fits or ⚠ Over. If over, it tells you how many sheets the layout would span — which is where tiling (below) comes in.

The layout size shown in inches is the real-world print size (the sum of your panel dimensions). It's mostly an FYI — the individual panel sizes are what matter for each decal.

11 · Export ZIP, PDF & what each gives you

Export ZIP bundles your choice of:

  • Individual Panels — each panel as its own full-resolution file. The core deliverable.
  • Layout Proof Sheet — a labeled overview of the whole kit for reference/approval.
  • Print Canvas Sheet — panels packed onto your chosen printer size.
  • Spec Sheet — dimensions and details for each panel.
  • Tiled / Mosaic — splits any panel bigger than your printer across multiple sheets with overlap, crop guides, and row/column labels for taping together. This is how you print big decals on an office printer.
  • Print-Ready PDF — one PDF with each panel on its own page, sized to its true print dimensions. Hand this straight to a print shop — every page is already the right size.
ZIP and PDF export need an internet connection (they load helper libraries). Everything else in CraneForge works fully offline.

Quick Links tray: the FEG-cube tab on the right edge of the Export box slides out a list of company links (ERP, Mail, P/Hub) that open in a new tab. Add your own links (vendors, references) with + Add — they save in your browser.

12 · Save, load & templates library, files, reuse

The 💾 Save ▾ button (top bar) has four options:

  • Save Kit — saves the whole kit (art, sizes, shapes, edits, crops, Merch Room) to the in-app library on this computer. Reopen it from the Saved Kits list that appears once you've saved one.
  • Save to File — downloads a portable .craneforge file to your computer. Keep it on your desktop, a share drive, or drop it in Google Drive. No size limit, and it moves between computers.
  • Save as Template — saves the layout only (panel sizes, shapes, positions) without the art. Use this once you've dialed in a machine's dimensions: load the template next time and just drop in fresh art. Never re-measure that machine again.
  • Load from File — open any .craneforge file, whether a full kit or a template.
Workflow tip: build one perfect layout for each machine you wrap, Save as Template, and reuse it for every seasonal theme. Save finished jobs with Save to File so they're archived and shareable.
The in-app library uses this browser's storage, which has a size limit — image-heavy kits may not fit. Save to File has no limit and is the safer choice for big kits or anything you need to keep.

Ctrl+Z undo · Ctrl+Y redo (50 steps) · Esc closes any popup.

13 · Project tabs several kits at once

The tab bar (above the Merch Room) lets you keep several projects open at once and switch instantly:

  • + New opens a fresh blank project — its own panels, art, Merch Room, sizes, everything.
  • Click a tab to switch to that project. The tab shows the kit name as you type it.
  • ✕ closes a tab (warns first if it has art). Up to 6 tabs at once.
Tabs are your live working set — they don't survive closing the browser on their own. To keep a project, use Save to File or Save Kit.
14 · Enlarge workstation big editable view

⛶ Enlarge (by the Sheet Preview) opens a full-screen editable workspace:

  • Merch Room docks across the top — drag art straight onto panels in the big view.
  • Panel list down the left — click a panel to expand its Edit Image controls and W/H sizing right there, one at a time.
  • The preview fills the rest, fit to screen with everything visible.
  • Close with the ✕ Close button (bottom-right), the backdrop, or Esc.
